Tolk Privacy Policy

Last updated: 22 November 2026

This policy explains which information Tolk uses and where it goes. In short: the text and speech you translate or dictate stay on your PC, unless you choose an online provider to clean up your dictation, then only the dictated text goes to that provider, or you choose Windows online speech recognition for dictation, then Windows sends your voice to Microsoft while you dictate. The only things it saves are your settings, your word list, and, if you keep that setting on, the transcripts of live translation, all on your own PC.

What stays on your PC

  • Screenshots and text. Tolk takes a screenshot only of the area you choose, reads the Dutch text in it, and translates it. All of this happens on your own PC. To fix reading mistakes, it can check the recognized words with the Dutch spell checker built into Windows, which also runs on your PC. Screenshots, the recognized text and the translations are kept in memory only, and are not saved or sent anywhere.
  • Sound (live translation). Only while you have live translation on, Tolk listens to the sound that other apps play on your PC, for example a video or a meeting. It does not use your microphone. It turns the Dutch speech into text, recognizes when the speaker changes, and translates it, all on your own PC. The sound and the voice fingerprints used to tell speakers apart are kept in memory only. They are not saved or sent anywhere, and they are gone when live translation stops.
  • Microphone (dictation). Only while you hold the dictation shortcut (or between two presses of it), Tolk records the microphone you chose (or your default one). It writes down what you say and cleans up the text, all on your own PC (unless you choose Windows online speech recognition or an online provider for the cleanup, see below), and pastes the text into the app you are typing in. To write it down, it uses a speech model you downloaded or, without one or if you choose it, the offline speech recognizer built into Windows (the one of Windows Speech Recognition), which also runs on your PC. The recording is kept in memory only. It is not saved or sent anywhere, and it is gone when the text is pasted. Windows shows the microphone icon while Tolk uses the microphone.
  • Microphone (start dictation by voice). Only if you turn on Start dictation by voice in Settings › Dictation (it is off by default), Tolk listens on the microphone all the time for a few spoken phrases, such as "Hey Tolk, write" and "Tolk, done". A small model on your PC listens for these phrases only; it does not write down anything else. Tolk keeps only the last 10 seconds of sound in memory, so a dictation can start with the words you say right after the phrase; older sound is overwritten and never saved or sent anywhere. It pauses while your PC is locked and, unless you turn that off, while another app uses a microphone, for example in a call. To do so, it reads which apps Windows lists as using a microphone, on your PC; it does not save or send this. Turn the setting off and Tolk closes the microphone at once.
  • Transcripts (live translation). With Save transcripts on (the default), Tolk saves the recognized Dutch and its English translation of each session as a text file on your PC, with the time and the speaker number. By default the files go to the "Tolk transcripts" folder in your Documents; you can choose another folder, or turn this off, in Settings. Tolk never sends these files anywhere, and does not delete them: they are yours to keep or delete. The sound itself is never saved.
  • Apps and devices. To offer the "Listen to" choice and the "Only on headphones" setting, Tolk reads which apps play sound and which sound devices are connected, on your PC. It does not save or send this list. Only the app you choose is saved, in the settings.
  • Volume of other apps. While live translation speaks the English, Tolk lowers the volume of the other apps on your PC, and puts it back when live translation stops. You choose how much in Settings, or turn it off. To put the volume back after a forced stop, Tolk keeps a small file on your PC with the names of the lowered apps and their volume, only while live translation runs.
  • Settings and word list. Your keyboard shortcuts, the chosen models, your live translation and dictation choices, the chosen text recognition, and the word list you write in Settings are saved on your PC, in the app's own data folder.
  • Clipboard. The app copies the English text to your clipboard only when you click Copy or press Ctrl+C. To paste dictated text, it puts the text on the clipboard for a moment, presses Ctrl+V, and then puts back what the clipboard held before. Dictated text is marked so that Windows' clipboard history and cloud clipboard leave it out.

Windows online speech recognition for dictation (only if you choose it)

If you turned on Online speech recognition in Windows Settings (Privacy & security › Speech), you can choose Windows online speech recognition as the speech model in Settings › Dictation. It is never used by default: when you choose it, Tolk first asks you, and says what will be sent, to whom, and how to stop. To stop, choose another speech model there, or turn the Windows setting off. Then:

  • What is sent. While you dictate, Windows records your default microphone and sends the sound of your voice to Microsoft's online speech service, which writes it down and gives the text back to Tolk. Tolk itself sends no sound; it records the microphone at the same time only to show how loud it is and to notice silence, and keeps that in memory only.
  • Where it goes. To Microsoft, as part of Windows' own online speech recognition. Microsoft's privacy statement applies, and the online speech recognition setting of Windows.
  • The text that comes back is handled like any dictated text: cleaned up on your PC, or by the online provider you chose for the cleanup, and pasted.

Online cleanup of dictation (only if you choose it)

Tolk cleans up dictated text on your own PC, with a small language model. On a slow PC you can choose an online provider to do this instead, in Settings › Dictation or Settings › Online providers. It is off by default, and nothing is sent until you choose a provider and agree: when you choose one, Tolk first asks you, and says what will be sent, to whom, and how to stop. To stop, choose On this PC under Settings › Dictation › Cleanup. Then:

  • What is sent. Speech recognition is not part of it: Tolk sends the provider no sound (only Windows online speech recognition, if you chose it, sends your voice, to Microsoft). After each dictation, Tolk sends the text it wrote down (with fillers and repeated words already taken out, and the fixes of your word list applied) and Tolk's own instructions for correcting it or turning it into a prompt. Nothing else from your PC is sent: no sound, screenshots, files, transcripts or other settings. The same happens when you click Make prompt in the main window, or Try it in Settings, which sends a fixed sample sentence.
  • Where it goes. To the provider you chose, under your own account with it and its own terms and privacy policy:
    • Claude, with Claude Code: Tolk starts Claude Code, which you installed and signed in to yourself, and Claude Code sends the text to Anthropic. Claude Code also sends a few details of its own, such as the date, the name of the (empty) folder it runs in, and the e-mail address of your Claude account.
    • ChatGPT, with Codex: Tolk starts the Codex CLI, which you installed and signed in to yourself, and Codex sends the text to OpenAI. Codex also sends a few details of its own, such as the date and the folder it runs in.
    • opencode: Tolk asks the opencode app on your PC for one answer, and opencode sends the text to the model you chose there: one of opencode's free models, through OpenCode's servers, or a model of an account you connected in opencode. Some of opencode's free models keep what you send and use it to improve the model; opencode lists which ones.
    • OpenRouter: Tolk sends the text to OpenRouter (openrouter.ai) with your API key, and OpenRouter passes it to the provider of the model you chose. Depending on the model and your privacy settings at OpenRouter, that provider may keep what you send or train on it; free models more often do.
  • No access to your files. Claude Code and Codex run without a window, in a new, empty temporary folder that Tolk deletes afterwards, with their own settings, plugins and tools switched off (Claude Code with no tools at all; Codex without its shell, browser, apps, plugins or other tools, and in its read-only sandbox). opencode is asked for a plain answer, without its agent or tools. So the model only gets the text, and cannot read or change your files.
  • Your OpenRouter key. Every user enters their own key; Tolk contains none. Tolk keeps it in Windows Credential Manager on your PC, where Windows encrypts it for your Windows account, and sends it only to OpenRouter, with each request and once to check it when you save it. Remove it with Remove key in Settings, or in Control Panel › Credential Manager › Windows Credentials (it is listed as Tolk/OpenRouter). Uninstalling Tolk does not remove it, so remove it first if you no longer want it on your PC.
  • The models you choose are saved in Tolk's settings on your PC. To list the models, Tolk asks Claude Code, Codex and opencode on your PC, and downloads OpenRouter's public list of models (without your key).

What uses the internet

The app connects to the internet to download its language files, when you ask for them, and, only if you choose an online provider for dictation, to clean up the dictated text as described above. If you choose Windows online speech recognition, Windows connects to Microsoft while you dictate, as described above. The downloads:

  • The translation model, from Hugging Face (huggingface.co).
  • The Dutch data for Tesseract, if you choose Tesseract, from GitHub (raw.githubusercontent.com).
  • The speech model for live translation, if you use it, from Hugging Face (huggingface.co) and GitHub (github.com).
  • The speech model and the cleanup model for dictation, if you use them, from Hugging Face (huggingface.co). With the speech recognizer built into Windows, dictation needs no speech model download.
  • The wake word model, if you turn on Start dictation by voice, from GitHub (github.com).
  • The graphics card support, if you turn on the NVIDIA graphics card in Settings, from NuGet (api.nuget.org) and NVIDIA (developer.download.nvidia.com).

For these downloads, your PC connects to those services the same way a web browser does. Like any web server, they see your IP address and the files requested. Tolk sends them no other information. Their own privacy policies apply to these connections.

After these files are downloaded, the app works without an internet connection, except for online cleanup of dictation and Windows online speech recognition, if you choose them.

What Tolk does not do

  • The developer does not receive your screenshots, screen text, audio, dictated text, or transcripts.
  • Tolk itself contains no analytics, tracking or advertising.
  • It does not send the text you translate, your screenshots, or the sound it hears, to any server. Dictated text goes only to the online provider you chose for dictation cleanup, if you chose one, and your voice only to Microsoft, through Windows, if you chose Windows online speech recognition.
  • It does not save sound. The microphone recording for dictation stays in memory until the text is pasted, and starting by voice keeps only the last 10 seconds in memory. Transcripts hold text only.

Tolk is built with Microsoft's .NET MAUI and Windows App SDK, which run as part of Windows. Their handling of diagnostic data follows Windows' own privacy settings and Microsoft's privacy statement.

Deleting your data

Uninstalling Tolk removes the app, its settings, its word list and its downloaded files. It does not remove an OpenRouter key you saved: remove it in Settings › Online providers before uninstalling, or later in Windows Credential Manager. Transcripts are in a folder you can see and choose, so they stay until you delete them yourself; Settings › Live translation › Open shows that folder. In Settings › Storage you can also remove downloaded files that you no longer use.
